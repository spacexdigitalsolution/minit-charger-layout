What is the Minit Charger Magnus?

Magnus is a universal DC fast charger built to serve mixed fleets — airport ground support equipment, buses, trucks, and passenger EVs — from a single platform. It outputs 24–1000 VDC at 100–200 kW, supports lead-acid, lithium, and EV battery chemistries, and includes intelligent power sharing and certified metering, so one charger can replace what would otherwise be two or three separate charging systems.

Reliability, by design

Don't engineer a single point of failure into your charging plan.

A one-voltage, one-chemistry charger isn't just inflexible — it's a constraint every future fleet, budget, and infrastructure decision has to work around. Here's where that constraint actually bites, and how Magnus is specified against it.

Failure mode

Voltage-class silos

A GSE tug goes electric this year, a shuttle next year, a truck the year after — and most platforms only serve one voltage class. You end up specifying a second charging system before the first is depreciated.

Design response

24–1000 VDC on one platform. Low-voltage GSE and high-voltage EVs charge from the same pedestal — no separate high-voltage system to capital-plan for later.

Failure mode

Chemistry lock-in

Lead-acid tugs, a lithium pilot, a passenger EV with its own onboard charger — procurement asks the same question every purchase cycle: will the charger even support this?

Design response

Chemistry-agnostic charging. Lead-acid, lithium, and EV packs all run from the same unit, so the charger stops constraining what you're allowed to buy.

Failure mode

Unattributed energy draw

Shared charging infrastructure across departments, tenants, or ground handlers usually means shared costs too — with no clean way to see which vehicle, shift, or operator actually drew the power.

Design response

Certified power metering with AssetPro-based reporting turns every session into billable, attributable data — not a shared utility line nobody can reconcile.

Failure mode

Feed capacity ceiling

Every new charger request turns into the same conversation with facilities: does the feed have enough capacity, and if not, whose capital budget absorbs the upgrade?

Design response

Intelligent power sharing on a single power feed allocates available power across connected chargers in real time — add charging capacity without necessarily adding electrical capacity.

Failure mode

The standalone-system island

A charger that can't talk to the BMS, SCADA, or ERP system your team already runs becomes one more screen to check, disconnected from the operational picture everything else feeds into.

Design response

Native SCADA / ERP integration plus cellular, Wi-Fi, Ethernet, and remote configuration — Magnus reports into the systems you already monitor, not a separate one.

Failure mode

Peak-demand penalty

A fleet that all charges at once during peak hours turns into the largest controllable line item on the utility bill — and nobody notices until the demand charge posts.

Design response

Off-peak and scheduled charging, combined with intelligent power sharing, shifts and balances load automatically instead of leaving it to chance.

Frequently asked questions

What buyers ask about Magnus.

What is the Minit Charger Magnus?
Magnus is a DC fast charger built to serve airport GSE, buses, trucks, and passenger EVs from a single platform, with an output range of 24–1000 VDC and 100–200 kW of power.
What connectors does Magnus support?
CCS1, CCS2, NACS, and GB/T for high-voltage EV charging, plus Anderson, Euro, and REMA connectors for traditional GSE and industrial equipment.
Can Magnus charge both low-voltage GSE and high-voltage EVs?
Yes. Magnus's 24–1000 VDC output range and multiple connector options let it serve traditional ground support equipment and high-voltage electric vehicles from the same unit.
Does Magnus support lithium and lead-acid batteries?
Yes, Magnus is chemistry-agnostic and supports lead-acid, lithium, and EV battery packs from the same charger.
What electrical input does Magnus require?
Magnus runs on 323–530 VAC three-phase input power, higher than Minit Charger's lower-voltage products, reflecting its expanded 100–200 kW output range. Confirm site power availability during a charging assessment.
Can Magnus be installed outdoors?
Yes, Magnus is rated for both indoor and outdoor pedestal installation.
Does Magnus support power sharing across multiple chargers?
Yes. Magnus includes intelligent power sharing, allocating available power across connected chargers on a shared feed in real time.
How is charging activity tracked and billed on Magnus?
Magnus includes certified power metering integrated with AssetPro 360 / Cumulus, so each charging session is recorded and attributable for reporting or billing.
Does Magnus integrate with airport SCADA, BMS, or ERP systems?
Yes. Magnus supports SCADA / ERP integration alongside cellular, Wi-Fi, and Ethernet connectivity and remote configuration, so it reports into the systems an airport engineering or facilities team already uses instead of running as a standalone system.
Can Magnus help reduce peak demand charges?
Magnus supports off-peak and scheduled charging combined with intelligent power sharing across connected units, which can help shift and balance charging load instead of concentrating it during peak utility demand periods.
